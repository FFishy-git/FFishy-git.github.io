I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Statistics and Data Science at Yale University, advised by Zhuoran Yang. Before Yale, I studied Electronic Engineering at Tsinghua University.

I work on large language models. My approach is to first understand how a model learns, and then use that understanding to train it better. Right now I focus on three things:

  • Foundations of learning, training dynamics and interpretability: how transformers pick up features and circuits during training, and when learning is possible at all.
  • Pretraining, scaling and latent reasoning: how data, architecture and optimizer choices shape what a model learns, how that changes with compute, and how models can reason in latent space.
  • Post-training and self-evolution: helping models improve their own reasoning through RL and curricula they write themselves.

I have interned at Google Research as a student researcher, and I am now a Qingyun intern at Tencent Hunyuan, working on omni-modal pretraining.

Beyond research, I enjoy the violin and classical music. Always happy to chat about research or anything else, so feel free to reach out.

Research Highlights

Foundations of Learning, Training Dynamics & Interpretability

  1. Taming Polysemanticity in LLMs: Theory-Grounded Feature Recovery via Sparse Autoencoders
    A new SAE training algorithm, Group-wise Bias Adaptation, with provable feature recovery. It finds cleaner, more interpretable features in real LLM activations.
    ICLR, 2026 · Invited talk, FAI Seminar 2025
  2. Unveiling Induction Heads: Provable Training Dynamics and Feature Learning in Transformers
    Shows end to end how training builds induction heads, the attention circuits behind in-context learning.
    NeurIPS, 2024 · Invited talk, FAI Seminar 2024
  3. Training Dynamics of Multi-Head Softmax Attention for In-Context Learning: Emergence, Convergence, and Optimality
    Tracks how multi-head attention trains and proves it converges to the optimal in-context learning algorithm.
    COLT, 2024 Oral · Invited talks, RLChina 2024 and FAI Seminar 2024
  4. Can Neural Networks Achieve Optimal Computational-Statistical Tradeoff? An Analysis on Single-Index Model
    Proves that neural networks trained by gradient descent reach the best possible sample complexity for single-index models, matching statistical-query lower bounds.
    ICLR, 2025 Oral
  5. An Optimized Franz–Parisi Criterion and Its Equivalence with SQ Lower Bounds
    Builds an optimized Franz–Parisi criterion from statistical physics and proves it is exactly equivalent to statistical-query lower bounds for high-dimensional inference.
    NeurIPS, 2025 Oral

Pretraining, Scaling & Latent Reasoning

  1. Truncate or Not? A Scaling Study of Looped Transformers with Truncated Backpropagation
    A scaling law for looped transformers over model size, training tokens, and forward and backward loop counts. At matched compute, it shows when full backprop through fewer loops beats truncation.
    Preprint, 2026 · co-first author
  2. Unlocking Out-of-Distribution Generalization in Transformers via Recursive Latent Space Reasoning
    A looped transformer that reasons in latent space and generalizes to inputs far outside its training range. Probing shows what each loop computes.
    Journal of Machine Learning Research · minor revision
  3. Towards Understanding Momentum Acceleration in River-Valley Loss Landscape
    Explains why momentum speeds up training on the river-valley loss landscapes seen in LLM pretraining.
    NeurIPS, 2026 · ICML 2026 HiLD Workshop Spotlight

Post-training & Self-Evolution

  1. INFUSER: Influence-Guided Self-Evolution Improves Reasoning
    A generator and a solver train together. The generator writes problems grounded in documents and learns which ones actually help the solver, using influence scores. The solver's reasoning improves as the curriculum evolves.
    NeurIPS, 2026 · Invited talk, NICETalk 2026
  2. GPT-Driven Drug Optimization with Structured Policy Optimization Post-training
    Post-trains a GPT model with a structured policy-optimization method to improve drug candidates.
    MLHC, 2026
